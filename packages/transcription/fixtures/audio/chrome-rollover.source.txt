chrome-rollover-{1,2,3}.webm — synthetic, no recorded voice (media-worker D.8).

Three consecutive MediaRecorder chunks from one getUserMedia stream, the recorder's rollover
(stop, then a new MediaRecorder on the same stream), recorded 2026-10-12 by headless Chromium
156.0.8078.4 (Playwright) with `--use-fake-device-for-media-stream
--use-file-for-fake-audio-capture=<a generated test-tone WAV, 48 kHz mono s16>`; `new MediaRecorder(stream,
{mimeType: 'audio/webm;codecs=opus', audioBitsPerSecond: 24000})`, `start(5000)`, stopped after
2.3 s; each chunk's slices joined into one Blob. Opus 48 kHz mono, 60 ms frames, 38 packets each.

Like every Chrome MediaRecorder WebM they have no duration header and their LAST block carries no
duration (mediabunny reports packet.duration 0), which is what made the packet-copy merge reuse
the last packet's timestamp for the next input's first packet (non-monotonic DTS at each seam).

chrome-rollover-drift-{1,2,3}.webm — the same three chunks' Opus packets, byte for byte, remuxed by
mediabunny into WebM with the block times a real microphone's clock gives Chrome: each block at
round(i × 59.6 ms) (steps of 0.059/0.060 s, as on a real capture; the fake device above is
sample-exact), the last block again without a duration. Against the old merge these reproduce the
real-device failure: the next chunk's first block lands 24 samples before the previous chunk's
decoded end ("Non-monotonic DTS; previous: 105864, current: 105840" with `-f wav -xerror`).
